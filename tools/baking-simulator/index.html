What's actually calculated?

Dough uses a 1D half-thickness finite-volume calculation; liquids and banana/oat suspensions use a well-mixed open-pan energy and water balance. Pulp/oat thickening, drying state and browning are qualitative estimates, not calibrated colour or charring predictions. Pure water is prevented from exceeding its approximated atmospheric boiling temperature while water remains. Interior heat conduction is coupled to moisture diffusion; surface vapor loss consumes latent heat. Material heat capacities and conductivities vary with composition and temperature. Gelatinization, protein setting and browning are approximate kinetic indicators, not laboratory-validated predictions. Gas production and bubble retention are empirical proxies; a pan-constrained effective-thickness approximation couples gas-driven expansion back to heat flow. A separate upper-end diagnostic estimates organic loss, char and mineral residue and an illustrative cracking-risk index. These estimates are not coupled to heat transfer, airflow or combustion. Neither solver includes validated fluid rheology, phase-separation kinetics, milk-skin mechanics, syrup viscosity, flames, free-loaf deformation, or microbiological safety. CO₂ escape is not deducted from the mass-loss estimate.
