# EXP-001 — Citrus Geode: competing food networks

**Status: Proposed — untested**  
**Theme:** Crystallization · bigels · enzymes · diffusion · plant-based formulations

## Scientific question
Can a composite with a wax-crystallized oil phase and a calcium-set pectin/water phase retain structure when a suitable pectin-depolymerizing enzyme selectively weakens the aqueous network?

## Concept
An orange-flavoured composite contains:
- **Oil network:** edible plant wax (candidate: candelilla) crystallized within sunflower oil.
- **Water network:** low-methoxyl pectin crosslinked by food-compatible calcium, using orange or neutral water as the liquid.
- **Activator:** a **characterized food-grade pectin-depolymerizing enzyme**, introduced in a later test.

Potential downstream extensions: local liquid release, structure-dependent diffusion, oxygen-sensitive colour change, and other induced phase transformations. None has been demonstrated in this specific system.

## Phase 0: verify the pieces independently
1. Produce a reproducible wax/oil gel with food-grade materials.
2. Produce a calcium/pectin gel; document pectin type and calcium form.
3. Confirm that the chosen enzyme degrades the pectin-only gel, comparing active enzyme and inactivated enzyme under the same conditions.
4. Confirm a stable combination can form without rapid oil/water separation.
5. Only then test whether enzymatic treatment alters the composite in a spatially localized way.

**Critical controls:** oil gel only; pectin gel only; composite with water; composite with inactivated enzyme; composite with active enzyme.

## Major uncertainties
- High wax/oil processing temperatures may not be compatible with all citrus flavour fractions.
- The oil and water phases may simply separate rather than make a connected bigel.
- Enzyme diffusion and pectin type determine whether any degradation occurs.
- Wax particles need not form a mechanically independent, continuous scaffold.
- A clearer juice fraction can have altered colloids, affecting gel formation.

## Success criterion for the first run
A coherent, repeatable composite plus a confirmed active-versus-inactivated-enzyme effect on an appropriate pectin control.

**No finished edible recipe or successful result is claimed yet.** See [experiment template](TEMPLATE.md) for formulation trials and recording real measurements.
