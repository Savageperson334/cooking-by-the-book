# Safety, food suitability and dietary requirements

**An experimental formulation is not automatically safe to eat.** Research papers describing food-related molecules can use temperatures, concentrations, solvents or handling methods that are unsuitable for eating.

- **Workplace lab boundary:** Materials processed in a chemical laboratory's shared centrifuge are **non-consumable demonstrations**, regardless of whether the starting materials were food. Food to be shared requires dedicated food-safe processing equipment and sanitary procedures.
- **Equipment:** Follow centrifuge/rotor-specific RCF, container, fill, sample density, balance and temperature limits; do not assume that a machine's maximum RPM applies to every rotor or bottle.
- **Ingredients:** Use food-grade ingredients and food-safe dose ranges for edible experiments; do not assume lab-grade enzymes, pigments, processing agents, or waxes are edible.
- **Microbiology:** Log preparation, storage, temperature and holding time. A stable-looking experimental gel is not proof of microbial safety.
- **Allergens and labelling:** Record nuts, soy, dairy, wheat and other relevant allergens. Do not assume plant-based means allergen-free.
- **Dietary compatibility:** Prefer plant-based formulations where feasible. For halal sharing, verify sourcing/certification of enzymes, emulsifiers, flavour carriers, additives and other processing aids as appropriate; 'plant-based' alone does not establish halal certification.
- **Scientific integrity:** Separate measured outcomes from speculation, and keep a failed experiment in the record.

Each experiment must say clearly whether its output is **food-safe intended**, **not yet validated**, or **non-consumable lab demonstration**.
