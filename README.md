# Cooking by the Book

**An open experimental cooking notebook.**

A place to cook, investigate, measure, break things, learn from them, and try again. That includes ordinary recipes, historic techniques, baking experiments, fermentation, flavour, plant chemistry, crystallization, centrifugation, and unusual food materials.

The point is **experimentation**, not a commitment to any one technique or prototype.

## How a test progresses

**Curiosity → question → source/hypothesis → small controlled test → observations → revision → repeat.**

Experiments can be as simple as comparing two batches of coffee or as ambitious as engineering a material that changes structure by itself.

Record the preparation *as performed*, not just the intended recipe. Keep unsuccessful trials and unexpected outcomes: that's where much of the learning happens.

## Principles

1. **Curiosity over scope:** investigate any interesting food-process question.
2. **Evidence over expectation:** distinguish published results, hypotheses, and what we actually observed.
3. **Repeatability over perfection:** use controls and record units, time, temperature, quantities, equipment and photos.
4. **Cooking still matters:** flavour, mouthfeel, accessibility and the pleasure of eating belong beside chemistry.
5. **Safe to share:** use food-grade, food-safe equipment for anything intended to be eaten. Consider halal compatibility and other dietary needs from the beginning.
